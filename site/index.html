YOUR EVERYDAY APPS

A few words. A familiar shortcut.

  1. 01

    Choose a field

    Click where you want to write, in a document, message, or text editor.

  2. 02

    Say what you mean

    Press Option-Space to start. Speak, then press it again to finish. Option-Escape cancels.

  3. 03

    Keep writing

    Dhwani copies the transcript. With Accessibility access, it can also paste into the original field.

Switching apps or fields stops automatic paste. Use Command-V to paste the text yourself.

SPEECH & PRIVACY

Apple speech.
Your settings.

Dhwani uses Apple's speech engine and requires an available on-device language model. Read the disclosure before allowing Speech Recognition access.

Read the speech & privacy disclosure ↗

No cloud-recognition fallback

If on-device recognition is unavailable, Dhwani does not start dictation.

You choose what gets saved

History, the latest-transcript file, and launch at login start off. Enable them in settings when you want them.

Your text goes to the clipboard

Dictation replaces your clipboard so you can use the text where you need it. Other apps can read clipboard contents.

A few common questions

Which Macs can use Dhwani?

The beta is for Apple Silicon Macs, M1 or newer, running macOS 14 or later. It requires an available Apple on-device model for your chosen speech language.

Why does macOS ask me to use Open Anyway?

This free beta is locally signed and has not been notarized by Apple. If macOS blocks the first launch, use Open Anyway for Dhwani in System Settings > Privacy & Security, if available. Some managed Macs do not allow this. Installation help

Do I need to give Accessibility access?

Only if you want automatic paste. Microphone and Speech Recognition access are needed to dictate. Without Accessibility, Dhwani copies the transcript and you paste it with Command-V.

Is Dhwani free and open source?

Yes. Dhwani is free to download, and its source is MIT licensed. You can browse the code, contribute, or build it yourself.
